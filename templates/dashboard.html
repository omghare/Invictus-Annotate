Tips for video datasets

Consecutive frames are nearly identical. Labelling 30 of them per second mostly repeats work and teaches the model nothing new — 1–3 fps is usually plenty.

Frames keep their order, so in the annotator you can turn on Carry boxes forward, or Propagate a box across many frames when the object doesn't move.

Need only part of a long recording? Trim it first in Video tools.

{{ icon('sparkle') }}Pre-label empty frames

The model draws its boxes on frames nobody has labelled yet. Annotators see them dashed, fix what's wrong and press Enter — usually several times faster than drawing from scratch. Frames stay “to do” until a person accepts them.

Higher = fewer, surer boxes. Lower = more boxes to delete.

How changes apply

Rename — existing boxes follow the new name.

Reorder — only the class ids in the exported labels change.

Remove — existing boxes are kept but marked as an unknown class. They show in grey in the annotator and are skipped at export until you re-add the class or relabel them.

Split

Keeping runs of neighbouring frames in the same split stops near-duplicate frames from appearing in both train and validation, which would make validation scores look better than they are.
